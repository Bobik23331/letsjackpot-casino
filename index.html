How the peg board actually works

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row wider than the one above it. A ball released at the top has roughly equal chances of moving left or right at each peg, and that choice repeats all the way down. Once it reaches the bottom, it lands in one of the available pockets, with the number of pockets always tied to the number of rows.

Because each bounce is a fresh left-or-right decision, results tend to cluster near the centre and reach the edges less often. Mathematicians call this a Galton board, and the pattern it creates follows a binomial curve. On Lets Jackpot betting games, that curve is turned into payout values: central pockets usually pay less, while rare edge landings pay more.

Six terms worth knowing

Rows
The number of peg lines the ball passes through; more rows mean more pockets and tighter odds.
